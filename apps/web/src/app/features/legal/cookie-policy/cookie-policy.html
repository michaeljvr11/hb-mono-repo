This page explains the cookies H&B E-Commerce uses and how to control them. It's the policy referenced by the cookie banner you see when you first visit the Platform.

What are cookies

Small files stored on your device that let a website remember information between visits.

What we use

Category Purpose Can you decline?
Strictly necessary Keep you signed in and keep the site secure. We set exactly two cookies: RefreshToken, an httpOnly cookie that keeps your session refreshed while you're signed in, and g_oauth_state, a short-lived (10-minute), httpOnly cookie that protects the Google sign-in round trip from cross-site request forgery. We currently run one consent-gated analytics integration (Google Analytics, not yet configured — see the table above) plus a first-party, non-cookie usage-events feature used for internal product analytics. That feature sets no cookies and collects no cross-site tracking data, which is why it isn't gated by this banner. It does store an anonymous session id in your browser's local storage — see "Browser storage we use that isn't cookies" below.

Browser storage we use that isn't cookies

Some parts of the Platform use your browser's local storage instead of cookies. Local storage isn't sent to us automatically with every request the way a cookie is, and none of the following identify you personally:

  • hb.analytics.sessionId — an anonymous, randomly generated id that groups your usage events together for the current visit. Never your name, email, or a persistent user id.
  • hb.consent.analytics — remembers the Accept/Decline choice you make on this banner, so we don't ask again on this device.
  • A sign-in session token, if you're logged in — lets the app keep you signed in without re-entering your password on every page.

Your choice

When you first visit, a banner lets you Accept or Decline analytics cookies. Your choice is stored on your device (see above) and applies until you change it. You can change your choice at any time by clearing this site's data in your browser settings, which resets the banner so you can choose again — a persistent "manage cookies" control elsewhere on the Platform isn't available yet.

Third-party cookies

Google Analytics, when enabled and accepted, sets its own cookies under Google's privacy policy (policies.google.com/privacy). We don't control what Google does with analytics data beyond what our GA configuration sends it.

For how we handle personal information more broadly, see our Privacy Policy.
